# CIP-0082 — Improved Rewards Scheme Parameters (4-stage)

> **Status:** Active 2026/04/23. Fee-layer reform (stages 1–2) + transversal `k` raise (stages 3–4). CIP identity and sources in §1; evaluation references in §3.4.

## Executive summary

- **Verdict.** **Not a standalone path to ratify.** CIP-0082 sequences four named stages: **Floor halving** (stage 1 — `minPoolCost: 340 → 170`, **already retired on mainnet** via Conway DIA.1.3), **Margin swap** (stage 2 — delete `minPoolCost`, add `minPoolRate = 3 %`, hard fork), and two **pool-count expansions** (stages 3–4 — `k: 500 → 750 → 1000`). Live content is the Margin swap + the two pool-count expansions. The evaluation of stages 3–4 as a standalone lever is handled in [`k-parameter.md`](k-parameter.md); this file's own structural critique (S1) targets the **Margin swap**.
- **Instrument.** Four stages — one parameter update (Floor halving, complete), one **hard fork** (Margin swap, pending HFC), two parameter updates (pool-count expansions). Proposed interval 3 epochs. Existing pool registration certificates preserved via $poolRateEff = \max(poolRate, minPoolRate)$.
- **One synthesis finding carries the Margin-swap verdict** — detailed in §3.1 and re-surfaced inline throughout:
  - **S1** — The Margin swap delivers on the delegator side and regresses on the operator side (3 F's: §3.1).
- **Stages 3–4** are analysed in [`k-parameter.md`](k-parameter.md) (k-lever.S1–S3). The CIP-0082-specific caveat: in the CIP's 3-epoch-interval timeline, the first pool-count expansion fires 6 epochs after the Margin swap — no stake-cap layer can be activated in that window, so the k-raise fires in exactly the regressive regime k-parameter.md identifies (§3.2).

## 1. Introduction

**CIP-0082 — "Improved Rewards Scheme Parameters".** A 2022 proposal to reform the reward-sharing scheme via four sequenced protocol actions: halve, then delete `minPoolCost`; introduce a proportional margin floor `minPoolRate`; raise the pool-count target `k` in two stages. The author frames the package as a fairness-and-decentralisation reform unlocking the pledge benefit that the current `minPoolCost` floor occludes.

### 1.2 Origin and context

**Authorship and moment.** Written in early 2022 by Tobias Fancee. The worked calibration in the CIP uses epoch-385 parameters (`minPoolCost = 340`, `a_0 = 0.3`, `k = 500`, rewards ≈ 31.2 M ₳/epoch, saturation ≈ 64 M ₳). The author's central observation: a 10 M ₳ community pool and a saturated pool both pay the same 340 ₳ flat fee, and the effective fee rate hyperbola penalises small productive pools. The CIP aggregates into one package three reforms the community had been discussing separately — `minPoolCost` reduction, `minPoolMargin` / `minPoolRate` introduction (crediting CIP-0023 explicitly), and `k` raises to amplify the pledge benefit.

**Stage 1 is already retired on mainnet.** In the Conway era, governance action DIA.1.3 halved `minPoolCost` from 340 to **170 ₳** — the exact change proposed in stage 1. The live content of CIP-0082 as a governance item is therefore **stages 2–4**. The CIP text preserves stage 1 as documentation; this evaluation treats it as a completed milestone and scopes the analysis to stages 2–4.

**Hard-fork requirement.** Stage 2 is the non-trivial change: a hard fork that deletes `minPoolCost`, introduces `minPoolRate`, and modifies the pool-fee calculation to $poolRateEff = \max(poolRate, minPoolRate)$ — a rule change preserving existing pool registration certificates without re-registration. Stages 3 and 4 are parameter updates only.

**Historical placement.** The CIP explicitly credits CIP-0023 for the margin-floor instrument: *"`minPoolRate` was originally proposed in CIP-0023."* CIP-0082 stage 2 is therefore mechanically equivalent to a *paired variant* of CIP-0023 (reduction of `minPoolCost` + introduction of a margin floor) — with the reduction taken to zero and the margin set to 3 % (vs CIP-0023's illustrative 50 ₳ + 1.5 %). The comparison with CIP-0023 paired is used as the structural reference in §3.1.

CIP-0082's primary weight lands on §3.1, which is why the operator-side revenue inversion (§3.1) is a load-bearing limit — not a tangential concern.

## 2. Mechanism

**Stage 2 formula** (post-hard-fork pool-fee calculation, replacing the prior `minPoolCost + poolRate × (R − minPoolCost)` shape):

$$\text{take}(\sigma) = poolRateEff_{i} \times R_{\text{pool}}(\sigma), \qquad poolRateEff_{i} = \max(poolRate_{i},\ minPoolRate)$$

With `minPoolCost` removed, the fixed-per-pool component vanishes entirely. Stages 3–4 leave the fee formula unchanged and modify only $k$ (and therefore $z_0 = \text{Supply}/k$).

**Design surface.**

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| New parameters | `minPoolRate` (= 0.03), `poolRateEff` (derived) |
| Deleted parameters | `minPoolCost` (at stage 2) |
| Changed parameters | `stakePoolTargetNum` (500 → 750 → 1000 across stages 3–4) |
| Hard fork required | Stage 2 only |
| Re-registration | Not required — `poolRateEff` derived in ledger reward calc |
| Layer | Fee (stages 1–2) + transversal (stages 3–4) |
| Reward curve shape | Untouched |
| Pledge mechanics | Untouched (amplified indirectly by k via $z_0$ shrinkage) |

### 2.1 Four stages and the worked calibration from the CIP rationale

CIP-0082 sequences four on-chain actions. Each has a distinct mechanical role, which is easier to hold in mind once named:

| # | Stage name | Action | What it does mechanically | Mainnet status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Floor halving** | `minPoolCost: 340 → 170 ₳` (parameter update) | Halves the flat per-pool fee. First-order relief on the $1/\sigma$ hyperbola without changing the fee *shape* — every pool still owes a fixed ADA amount | **Complete on mainnet** — Conway governance action DIA.1.3 |
| 2 | **Proportional-margin swap** | Delete `minPoolCost`; add `minPoolRate = 3 %` (hard fork + ledger-rule change) | Replaces the flat-ADA floor with a size-proportional floor via $poolRateEff = \max(poolRate, minPoolRate)$. The effective fee *rate* becomes constant at 3 % across every pool size. This is CIP-0023's *paired* variant at harsher calibration | Pending HFC |
| 3 | **First pool-count expansion** | `k: 500 → 750` (parameter update) | Shrinks saturation from $z_0 \approx 77$ M ₳ to $\approx 51$ M ₳. Author's stated goal: "get stale delegations moving" by oversaturating large pools and amplifying the pledge benefit via $a_0$ on the reward curve | Pending |
| 4 | **Second pool-count expansion** | `k: 750 → 1000` (parameter update) | Further shrinks saturation to $\approx 38.5$ M ₳. Completes the concentration-relief programme: every current Large-healthy pool is reclassified as Near-saturation or beyond at the new cap | Pending |

**Per-stage role in one line each.** Stage 1 *shrinks* the flat floor. Stage 2 *replaces* it. Stages 3 and 4 *fragment the top of the distribution* that the replaced floor was protecting — making them coherent only if the underlying pledge signal is simultaneously restored by a stake-cap layer (§3.3).

**Headline figures (author's own).** Three summary metrics distilling each stage's effect on the *delegator side*. All three describe how the fee scheme treats a *pledge-only pool* — one whose stake equals its pledge, before any external delegation arrives.

- **Viability point (pledge).** The smallest pledge at which a zero-delegation pool produces *any* delegator reward at all. Below this threshold, the pool's epoch reward is smaller than `minPoolCost`, so the entire reward is absorbed by the flat fee and delegators receive zero. In practice this is the floor an operator must clear before registering a pool is not immediately futile.
- **Competitive point (pledge).** The smallest pledge at which a zero-delegation pool's delegator ROS matches the ROS of an already-saturated, zero-pledge pool. Below this pledge, a rational delegator is better off joining a saturated pool than a small pledged one — the small pool cannot compete on yield. Above it, the pledged small pool is finally yield-competitive with the incumbent fleet and can in principle attract organic delegation.
- **Delegator ROS dispersion (100 K pool → saturated).** The spread between a tiny 100 K-ADA pool's delegator ROS and a saturated pool's, holding all else equal. This is the single-number summary of the OPE.O1.F4 / OPE.O5.F1 regressivity — how much the fee scheme penalises delegators to small pools. A high value means delegator yield depends strongly on pool size; a low value means pool size is largely irrelevant to delegator return.

| Headline | Current | Stage 1 — Floor halving | Stage 2 — Margin swap | Stage 3 — k → 750 | Stage 4 — k → 1000 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Viability point (pledge) | ~670 K ₳ | ~335 K ₳ | 1 ₳ | 1 ₳ | 1 ₳ |
| Competitive point (pledge) | ~20 M ₳ | ~16.5 M ₳ | 1 ₳ | 1 ₳ | 1 ₳ |

**Reading — row by row.**

*Row 1 — Viability point.* An operator needs ~670 K ₳ of self-pledge today to register a pool that will pay its delegators anything at all (the **Floor halving** cuts that to ~335 K ₳ on mainnet — already in force). Under the **Margin swap** `minPoolCost` disappears, so the flat-fee trap vanishes with it: any pledge ≥ 1 ₳ produces a nonzero delegator reward. The viability threshold becomes numerically trivial. (Note: trivial *arithmetic* viability does not imply *economic* viability for the operator — see §3.2.)

*Row 2 — Competitive point.* Under today's scheme, a fresh pool needs ~16.5 M ₳ of self-pledge before its zero-delegation ROS matches a saturated hollow pool's — a prohibitive barrier for any operator without multi-million-ADA capital sitting in self-custody. Under the **Margin swap**, every pool pays the same flat 3 % fee rate, so delegator ROS is invariant to pool size, and any ≥ 1 ₳ pledged pool is instantly "competitive" on yield with a saturated pool. The organic-delegation path the current scheme closes for small operators is opened — *on paper*, subject to the OPE.O7.F1 caveat in §3.2.

*Row 3 — Delegator ROS dispersion.* The yield gap between a 100 K ₳ pool and a saturated pool shrinks from **2.53 pp today to 0.016 pp** under the **Margin swap** — a ~160× compression. This is the headline delegator-side outcome of the reform: fee-driven differences in delegator return disappear, and the residual 0.016 pp is the pledge benefit ($a_0 = 0.3$) rewarding the 1 M-pledge saturated pool slightly more than the 100 K-pledge baseline.

**Why the dispersion drifts *back up* at stages 3–4.** The **pool-count expansions** do not change the fee rule — the 3 % margin still applies everywhere, so the fee-regressivity component stays at zero. What the k-raises do is shrink $z_0 = \text{Supply}/k$, which makes the pledge-benefit coefficient $a_0$ bite harder per unit of pool reward. The saturated 1 M-pledge pool pulls slightly further ahead of the 100 K-pledge baseline — 0.016 → 0.024 → 0.031 pp. This is the *intended* effect: the author states explicitly that the k-raises are there to "increase the effect of the pledge benefit on rewards". The dispersion creep is not a regression of the fee scheme; it's pledge differentiation activating more strongly, by design.

**Why saturated-pool operator revenue falls 1081 → 720 → 543 across stages 2 → 3 → 4.** The pool-count expansions also halve saturation each time. Total reward pot is unchanged; more pools are sharing the same pot, so the per-pool reward at saturation shrinks. The saturated-pool operator's 3 % share shrinks with it — 1081 ₳/ep at $k = 500$, 720 at $k = 750$, 543 at $k = 1000$. This has nothing to do with the fee mechanism; it is arithmetic on the $1/k$ saturation size.

**What the three rows together do not show.** All three metrics look at the *delegator side*. The corresponding *operator-side* picture — absolute ADA revenue per pool under the Margin swap — runs in the opposite direction: the 3 % margin produces a revenue stream that scales linearly with pool size, inverting the transfer across the viability line. This is the structural issue quantified in §3.1.

### 2.2 Updated calibration at current parameters (epoch ~623, 2026/04)

Stage 1 is retired (mainnet `minPoolCost = 170`; see §1.2). Stages 2–4 are the forward-looking content. Calibration below uses today's parameters: $z_0 = 77$ M ₳ at $k = 500$, hollow-pool yield at saturation ≈ 2.275 %/yr (≈ 24 000 ₳/epoch at saturation), $a_0 = 0.3$, total reward pot $P \approx 12$ M ₳/epoch. Representative σ per tier follows the canonical nine-tier taxonomy from [pools-distribution §4.1.3](../../diagnostic/sub-flows/pools-distribution/mainnet-analysis/README.md#413-tier-definitions). All figures below assume a **hollow pool** (`poolRate ≈ 0`, pledge ≈ 0), matching the CIP's own worked calibration.

The fee-rate picture would show 3 % everywhere from the Margin swap onward — not useful. The two axes that actually move across stages are **delegator net ROS** and **operator annualised revenue**, and they move in opposite directions. Both are shown below.

**How the reward floor moves with k.** Below saturation, per-ADA gross yield is constant at ≈ 2.275 %/yr regardless of σ or $k$ (the reward curve is size-proportional). *Above* saturation the per-pool reward caps at $P/k$ — so every k-raise *halves* the effective reward-per-ADA for pools stranded above the new cap. At $k = 500$, $P/k = 24\,000$ ₳/ep. At $k = 750$, $16\,000$. At $k = 1\,000$, $12\,000$. Pools above the new $z_0$ see their yield — and the operator's 3 % cut of it — shrink one-for-one.

**Saturation cap per stage:**

| Stage | $k$ | $z_0 = \text{Supply}/k$ | Saturation-pinned reward $P/k$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Current / Margin swap | 500 | ~77 M ₳ | 24 000 ₳/ep |
| Stage 3 — k → 750 | 750 | ~51.3 M ₳ | 16 000 ₳/ep |
| Stage 4 — k → 1000 | 1000 | ~38.5 M ₳ | 12 000 ₳/ep |

#### Delegator net ROS across the nine tiers (%/yr)

Net ROS = gross yield × (1 − effective fee rate). Gross yield is 2.275 %/yr below saturation; above saturation it is $(P/k) \cdot 73 / \sigma$ and drops with larger pools.

| Canonical tier | Rep. σ | Current (`minPoolCost = 170`) | Stage 2 — Margin swap | Stage 3 — k → 750 | Stage 4 — k → 1000 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Zero-stake | 0 | — | — | — | — |
| Dormant | 50 K | **0 %** (fee consumes pool) | 2.21 % | 2.21 % | 2.21 % |
| Sub-production | 500 K | **0 %** (fee consumes pool) | 2.21 % | 2.21 % | 2.21 % |
| Sub-viable | 2 M | 1.65 % | 2.21 % | 2.21 % | 2.21 % |
| Healthy | 15 M | 2.19 % | 2.21 % | 2.21 % | 2.21 % |
| Large healthy | 50 M | 2.25 % | 2.21 % | 2.21 % (still ≤ $z_0$) | **1.70 %** (now above $z_0$) |
| Near-saturation | 67 M | 2.26 % | 2.21 % | **1.69 %** (now above $z_0$) | **1.27 %** |

| Oversaturated | 85 M | 2.05 % | 2.00 % | **1.33 %** | **1.00 %** |

*Reading.* The **Margin swap** is a clean win at the bottom of the distribution: Dormant / Sub-production jump from 0 % to 2.21 %; Sub-viable jumps from 1.65 % to 2.21 %. Above the Healthy tier, delegator ROS barely changes (2.21 % vs current 2.19–2.26 %). Most delegated pools on mainnet sit in Healthy or above, so most delegators see **a 0.02–0.05 pp shift, not the headline jump**. The two **pool-count expansions** do not touch the fee mechanics but clip gross yield at the top of the distribution — a delegator at today's saturated pool sees ROS drop from 2.26 % to 1.10 % across stages 2 → 4 because per-pool reward at the new cap is now 12 000 ₳/ep rather than 24 000. This is not a fee effect; it is $P/k$ arithmetic.

> **Finding CIP-0082.S1.F1 [D] — Delegator fee-rate dispersion collapses from 38× to 1.00×.** Under the Margin swap, the net-ROS floor rises from 0 % / 1.65 % (Sub-viable and below, today) to 2.21 % (flat across the productive range). This is a direct delivery on the CIP's stated §3.3 delegator-yield claim — though the gain is concentrated in the tiers that currently attract the *fewest* delegators (see CIP-0082.S2.F2 for the redistribution caveat).

#### Operator annualised revenue across the nine tiers (₳/yr)

| Canonical tier | Rep. σ | Current (`minPoolCost = 170`) | Stage 2 — Margin swap | Stage 3 — k → 750 | Stage 4 — k → 1000 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Zero-stake | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Dormant | 50 K | 1 139 (pool absorbed) | 34 | 34 | 34 |
| Sub-production | 500 K | 11 373 (pool absorbed) | 341 | 341 | 341 |
| Sub-viable | 2 M | 12 410 | **1 365** | 1 365 | 1 365 |
| Healthy | 15 M | 12 410 | 10 242 | 10 242 | 10 242 |
| Large healthy | 50 M | 12 410 | **34 128** | 34 128 (≤ $z_0$) | **26 280** (above new $z_0$) |

*Reading.* Today's flat `minPoolCost = 170 ₳` caps every productive pool's operator revenue at **12 410 ₳/yr** — the signature of OPE.O6.F4 (no single-pool operator earns a competitive wage). The **Margin swap** replaces the flat cap with a proportional share of the pool reward: Sub-viable operators fall from 12 410 to 1 365 ₳/yr (a 9× *cut*), while Saturated operators jump from 12 410 to 52 560 ₳/yr (a 4× *multiplication*). **The operator-revenue curve inverts across the viability line** — the delegator-side equalisation is funded by a transfer up the operator distribution. The **pool-count expansions** then compress operator revenue at the top of the distribution as $P/k$ shrinks (52 560 → 35 040 → 26 280 at saturation) while leaving the Sub-viable, Sub-production, and Dormant tiers unchanged at their Margin-swap values. Stages 3–4 *do not help the small-pool tail at all*; they redistribute revenue *among large pools only*.

> **Finding CIP-0082.S1.F2 [R] — Per-pool operator revenue inverts across the viability line.** Sub-viable operators go from 12 410 to 1 365 ₳/yr (–9×); Saturated operators go from 12 410 to 52 560 ₳/yr (+4×). The CIP-0023 paired variant produces the same inversion with smaller magnitudes (~2× smaller each side). This is the operator-side counterpart of S1.F1 — the delegator-side equalisation is *funded* by a transfer from small operators to large ones.

Per-pool $R(\sigma)$ computed at each stage's $z_0$.

*Reading.* At each bracket's *mean* pool stake, pools stay below $z_0$ at every k-raise — 38.24 M < 38.5 M (the $k = 1000$ cap) for the 2-pool MPO; comfortably below for the other three. So the **pool-count expansions do not erode MPO entity revenue**. The Margin-swap uplift — **+27 K** ₳/yr for a 2-pool MPO to **+200 K** for an 11+ pool MPO — is preserved through all later stages. The revenue trimming at the top of the nine-tier table (Large-healthy / Near-saturation / Saturated collapsing from 52 560 to 26 280 ₳/yr across stages 2 → 4) applies to *individual high-stake pools*, not to the mean MPO pool. The k-raises clip the tails of the single-pool distribution; they leave MPO fleets whole.

*Caveat on fleet heterogeneity.* Some individual pools inside an MPO fleet do sit at or near today's saturation cap (Everstake, for instance, operates individual pools above 50 M ₳); those specific pools do see reward-cap compression across stages 3 → 4. But on the bracket *mean* — the operator-level aggregate — revenue is insulated from the k-raises.

> **Finding CIP-0082.S1.F3 [R] — On the n-MPO fleet axis, the transfer compounds mechanically with fleet size.** +27 K ₳/yr for a 2-pool MPO entity; +200 K ₳/yr for an 11+ pool MPO — a 7.3× bracket-to-bracket amplification. The transfer direction (up-distribution) is preserved through stages 3–4 because MPO mean pool stakes stay below $z_0$ at every k-raise. This closes the S1 argument: the Margin swap's delegator-side equalisation is funded primarily by the small-pool tail, and captured primarily by the large-fleet top.

**Putting it together.** The Margin swap buys the delegator-side equalisation (ROS floor at 2.21 %) at the cost of an operator-side regression (small-pool operator revenue cut ~9×). The subsequent k-raises leave the delegator-side gain intact for small pools but damage it for pools above the shrinking $z_0$ — and on the operator side, they trim the top of the transfer without returning any of it to the tiers the CIP's primary V2 §3.1 claim says to protect. The full quantification of the cross-tier and cross-fleet operator-revenue transfer is in §3.1.

## 3. Limits of CIP-0082 as a standalone proposal

Three structural limits — one per active-stage grouping of the remaining content.

### 3.1 Margin swap inverts operator revenue across the viability line — more aggressively than CIP-0023

The structural effect is the same — delegator-side fee dispersion collapses; operator-side per-pool revenue scales with $R(\sigma)$ and the transfer inverts across the viability line — but the magnitude is roughly doubled on both sides.

#### 3.1.3 Reading

This is the same structural critique CIP-0023 paired faces ([see cip-0023.md §3.2.3](cip-0023.md#323-reading--why-this-fails-the-missing-cps)) scaled up roughly ~4×. The scaling follows from the choice of `minPoolCost = 0` + `minPoolRate = 3 %` vs CIP-0023 paired's 50 + 1.5 %.

### 3.2 Pool-count expansions — standalone `k`-lever analysis in [`k-parameter.md`](k-parameter.md)

CIP-0082 stages 3–4 raise `k` from 500 → 750 → 1000 without changing the reward formula. The standalone evaluation of that lever — its mechanical effect on the operator / delegator split, its delegator-market assumptions, and its structural limits — lives in a companion file, [`k-parameter.md`](k-parameter.md). A summary of the three synthesis findings established there, reproduced here for reference:

| Code | Tag | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| **k-lever.S1** | mixed | A standalone `k`-raise compresses the top of the distribution and amplifies the pledge bonus for self-pledged pools; hollow pools below saturation are mechanically unchanged, and the production + viability thresholds are k-invariant (4 F's) |
| **k-lever.S2** | R | A fine tool for a narrow delegator segment, and insufficient on the operator side because the reward formula is unchanged — the ROS-responsive segment is ≈ 8 % of productive stake at best (CEN.O4.F2, CEN.O6.F1, CEN.O6.F3), and the non-pledge equilibrium (POL.O2.F2) is preserved (2 F's) |
| **k-lever.S3** | R | Under the current weak-pledge regime, new pool slots are absorbed horizontally by existing MPO fleets — historical precedent (`k: 150 → 500` → today's POL.O4.F1 landscape) plus structural economics (registration cost trivial vs MPO revenue, pledge non-binding on expansion) (2 F's) |

**What CIP-0082 adds on top of the standalone critique.** CIP-0082 stages 3–4 fire **6 epochs after the Margin swap** (stage 2) in the CIP's own 3-epoch-interval timeline — no stake-cap layer can be negotiated, voted, and activated in that window. The k-raise therefore fires **in exactly the regime `k-parameter.md` identifies as regressive**: weak pledge, MPO-dominated fleet, empirically non-yield-following delegator base. CIP-0082 does not embed the precondition the lever needs to flip from regressive to constructive (an active stake-cap layer — CIP-0050's `L` or CIP-0037's dynamic saturation). Stages 3–4 are therefore not safe to fire in the sequence CIP-0082 proposes. **The package either needs to acquire a stake-cap layer between stage 2 and stage 3 or to remove stages 3–4.**
