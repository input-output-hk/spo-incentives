## Abstract

The reward pipeline's long-term viability rests on a single assumption: *that transaction fees will eventually replace monetary expansion as the dominant source of the epoch pot.* Today, fees contribute approximately **0.19%** of the pot. Reaching self-sufficiency would require a **12–16× increase** in fee revenue at current transaction volumes. **The submitter population is moving in the opposite direction**: distinct fee-paying addresses contracted from a peak of **790,335 (epoch 304) to 30,505 (epoch 623)** — a **96% decline**.

The most fee-intensive segment of that population — script-based transactions paying **2.4×** the average per-tx rate — is dominated by enterprise and script addresses that **structurally cannot participate in delegation**. *The population that the mechanism needs to grow is shrinking; the sub-population that generates the most revenue per transaction is excluded from the rewards it funds.* The gap is shaped by three converging forces: a contracting submitter population, a fee base that concentrates rather than diversifies, and a growing share of fee revenue generated by addresses outside the staking system.

This CPS formally defines the **fee-input insufficiency** problem at the transaction-submitter layer.
