Solution Evaluation · Fee Layer

CIP-0023 — Fair Min Fees

minPoolMargin floor — subsumed by CIP-0082 stage 2
2026/04/30 · Nicolas Henin

CIP-0023 introduces a single new parameter — minPoolMargin, a floor on the variable-fee margin that operators charge delegators. It is a fee-layer instrument: the V1 reward formula and per-pool allocation are left untouched; only the operator/member split after the allocation changes. The proposal is the historical anchor for the margin-floor concept and predates the broader CIP-0082 reform.

The intent is to make small pools more ROS-attractive to delegators by setting a floor under operator margin — preventing a race-to-zero where small operators undercut each other on margin to the point that none of them can cover their fixed cost. As an instrument it is conservative: one new scalar parameter, no hard fork, no re-registration, no change to existing pool certificates.

The catch is structural. CIP-0082 stage 2 reproduces the same intent inside a larger staged package (with a rate-floor primitive that subsumes the margin-floor mechanic). As a live candidate today, CIP-0023 is dominated by CIP-0082 stage 2 unless governance explicitly declines the hard fork that stage 2 requires.

The core question this evaluation asks: under what conditions does CIP-0023 add value beyond CIP-0082, and under what conditions does it introduce regressive transfers up the distribution?

The argument proceeds in three parts:

  1. Introduction (§1). CIP identity card, origin (2021 — Lujhön Sotelo), the diagnostic findings around small-operator viability and ROS convergence the proposal targets, and the V2 §3 problem statement (Missing CPS) it implicitly addresses.

  2. Mechanism (§2). The fee-split formula with the new minPoolMargin floor, worked calibration at current mainnet parameters, the regime where the floor binds, and the per-pool effects across the canonical 9-tier taxonomy.

  3. Limits as a standalone proposal (§3). Three synthesis findings — S1 (functional redundancy with CIP-0082 stage 2 — Regresses), S2 (partial CPS coverage with regressive leakage up the distribution — Regresses), S3 (blind spot at the below-floor tail where the instrument does not reach the population it is meant to help — Blind spot).

The Executive summary below packages the verdict; the Findings summary table after it is the navigation index for §3.

Executive summary

  • Verdict. Not recommended as a standalone proposal to ratify. CIP-0023 is a historical reference point for the margin-floor concept; as a live item in the current review cycle it is dominated by CIP-0082 stage 2 (which reproduces the same instrument inside a larger staged package) and it addresses only half of the reconstructed CPS (§1.4) — ROS convergence across pool sizes — while leaking operator-side revenue to pools that have no viability problem.
  • Instrument. One new parameter, minPoolMargin (variable-fee floor), applied inside the ledger's reward calculation after minPoolCost. No hard fork, no re-registration.
  • Three synthesis findings carry the verdict — detailed in the next section and cross-referenced throughout:
  • S1 — Functionally redundant with CIP-0082 stage 2; no additional mechanism, doubled governance surface (1 F: §3.1).
  • S2 — Partial CPS coverage: the margin floor leaks revenue up the distribution — regressively on both the pool axis and the operator-fleet axis (3 F's: §3.2).
  • S3 — Blind spot at the below-floor tail: Dormant and Sub-production tiers stay 100 % fee-consumed; the instrument does not reach the population it is nominally meant to help (1 F: §2.2).

1. Introduction

CIP-0023 — "Fair Min Fees". A 2021 proposal to introduce a floor on the variable-fee component of the operator take (minPoolMargin), targeting the regressive effect of the existing fixed pool cost on delegators to small pools.

1.2. Origin and context

Authorship and moment. Written in early-Shelley era, February 2021. The worked example in the CIP uses epoch-382 parameters, with minPoolCost at 340 ADA and a0 at 0.3. The authors' observation: a 100 k ADA delegator faced an effective fee rate of 22.7 % in a 2 M ADA pool versus 0.7 % in a saturated 64 M ADA pool — a 32× dispersion driven almost entirely by the fixed cost component. The CIP framed this as a fairness problem — delegators penalised for spreading stake across smaller productive pools, and small operators structurally disadvantaged.

Scope the CIP explicitly excludes. The text is explicit: "this proposal does not change or reduce the existing minimum fixed pool fee." The CIP proposes the margin-floor instrument only. Any reduction in minPoolCost is left to a separate governance action. This self-imposed scope restriction is load-bearing for the evaluation — it is why the paired variant in §2.2 and §3.2 is labelled out-of-scope for the standalone CIP.

Historical placement. Written before Conway-era governance and before CIP-0082 — whose stage 2 reproduces the margin-floor mechanism under a different name (minPoolRate). CIP-0023 is therefore both a live technical proposal and a historical reference point for the margin-floor concept that CIP-0082 later operationalises within a staged path. §3.1 resolves the current relationship with CIP-0082.

1.3. Related diagnostic findings

CIP-0023 is a fee-layer instrument acting on the operator/delegator split after per-pool allocation. Its rationale attacks two problems at once: the regressive burden on delegators of small productive pools, and the structural disadvantage of the operators running them. Two findings from the operator-delegator-distribution mainnet analysis (../../diagnostic/sub-flows/operator-delegator-distribution/mainnet-analysis/README.md) are the CIP's direct targets.

1.4. Missing CPS — mapping to V2 §3.1

CIP-0023 ships without a formal Cardano Problem Statement. Read for what it actually points at, the CIP diagnoses a single mechanical defect: return-on-stake is structurally unfair between small and large pools for the same delegator. A delegator placing 100 k ADA in a 2 M pool keeps a far smaller share of that pool's reward than a delegator placing 100 k ADA in a saturated pool — because the flat minPoolCost is deducted before the margin, so it consumes a disproportionate share of a small pool's reward and only a trivial share of a saturated pool's. The instrument introduced to ensure operator sustainability at the bottom of the distribution has inverted its purpose at the delegator layer: it makes small pools less attractive, not more.

The reconstructed CPS statement therefore reads:

The operator growth path is not functioning as intended. Small productive pools cannot accumulate the delegation they need to reach viability, because the protocol's own fixed-cost floor — intended to finance their operator — makes them strictly less attractive on net ROS than the large pools they are supposed to grow into. The instrument designed to support the small-pool layer has turned delegator mobility against the operators it was meant to protect.

This maps directly onto the foundational V2 specification — §3.1 Guarantee operator viability across the entire productive population:

CPS slot What V2 §3.1 provides — standing in for CIP-0023's missing CPS
Problem statement The mechanism does not guarantee a delegation-mobility path from small productive pools to viable ones. The flat minPoolCost is paid out of the pool reward before the margin; at small pool sizes it dominates, making those pools uncompetitive on net ROS and stranding their operators below viability

Why this is not "just a delegator problem". The CIP's own rationale is written in delegator-facing language ("remove the regressive effect of minPoolCost on delegators"). V2 §3.1 frames it in operator-facing terms (viability, growth path). Both are correct — they are two views of the same asymmetry: the fixed-cost floor couples delegator mobility and operator viability through a single mechanical channel, and fails on both sides simultaneously.

In both variants, the two below-floor tiers (Dormant and, under standalone, Sub-production) remain fully consumed by the fee — the instrument does not help pools whose reward cannot reach the floor, regardless of margin calibration.

Dormant and Sub-production (pools whose epochal reward doesn't reach the 170 ₳ floor) are untouched by today's parameters and by the standalone variant — 100 % of pool reward absorbed by the flat fee. Even the out-of-scope paired variant relieves only Sub-production (100 % → 33 %) and leaves Dormant at 100 %. The instrument does not reach the struggling-pool tail it is nominally meant to help.

3. Limits of CIP-0023 as a standalone proposal

Two structural limits converge on the same verdict: CIP-0023 is better read as the historical seed of the margin-floor concept than as a live proposal to ratify today.

3.1. Functional redundancy — CIP-0082 stage 2 reproduces the same instrument

CIP-0082 stage 2 removes minPoolCost and introduces minPoolRate — mechanically identical to CIP-0023's minPoolMargin. The CIP-0082 author explicitly credits CIP-0023 for the concept. CIP-0082 then adds three further stages that reshape the fee layer end-to-end (drop of minPoolCost, rate progression, k increases), where CIP-0023 stops at the single instrument.

Scenario Outcome
CIP-0023 ships first Stage 2 of CIP-0082 effectively renames and formalises minPoolMargin — minPoolMargin becomes a de-facto placeholder for minPoolRate
CIP-0082 ships first CIP-0023 is strictly redundant — stage 2 already provides the margin floor, subsequent stages extend it
Both enacted as separate items Incoherent — same instrument under two names, governance surface doubled, parameter calibration coupled across two CIPs

Implication. Treating CIP-0023 and CIP-0082 as independent options forces a false choice — they are not independent. A coherent review cycle picks CIP-0082 (which contains CIP-0023's instrument) and deprecates CIP-0023 as superseded (CIP-0023.S1).

Finding CIP-0023.S1.F1 [R] — Same instrument, two governance items. CIP-0082 stage 2's minPoolRate is mechanically identical to CIP-0023's minPoolMargin, and the CIP-0082 author credits CIP-0023 explicitly. Ratifying both as separate items doubles the governance surface (two on-chain parameter changes, two calibration decisions, two review cycles) without delivering any additional mechanism — the CIP's core contribution (the margin-floor concept) is preserved intact inside CIP-0082 stage 2.

3.2. Partial coverage of the missing CPS — the instrument leaks revenue toward pools with no viability problem

The reconstructed CPS at §1.4 has two sides: ROS convergence across pool sizes (delegator layer) and income support for the operators of small pools that cannot yet attract delegation (operator layer). CIP-0023 addresses the first and actively mis-allocates the second: a proportional margin floor of 1.5 % produces a per-pool operator-revenue change that scales with pool size, so the same mechanism meant to help the small-pool tier redirects a far larger ADA amount to operators that already have no viability problem — and the mis-allocation is amplified when the same operator runs many pools.

3.2.3. Reading — why this fails the missing CPS

Standalone variant (minPoolCost unchanged at 170, margin floor 1.5 %):

  • The small-pool delegator effective rate barely moves (§2.2: 27.3 % → 28.4 %). Delegators of the pools the CIP is supposed to help see no meaningful ROS improvement.
  • The mechanism is a delegator-to-operator transfer, not a small-pool-to-large-pool redistribution reversal — and inside that transfer, large operators accumulate the largest share because (a) Δ per pool grows with σ (pool axis), and (b) MPO entities multiply Δ across their fleet (operator axis). The two amplifications compound: 502× gap between the 11+ pool MPO entity and the sub-viable single-pool operator.
  • Sub-production and Dormant pools — the struggling-pool tail the reform gestures at — receive zero uplift, because their pool reward does not reach the unchanged 170 ₳ floor.

Paired variant (minPoolCost = 50, margin floor 1.5 %):

  • Solves the delegator-side ROS problem: fee-rate dispersion collapses from 38× to 5.5× (§2.2).
  • But the operator-side path is inverted across the viability line: sub-viable and viable-but-small operators lose 50–112 ₳/epoch per pool; Large-healthy and above gain 113–239 ₳/epoch. On the entity axis, every MPO bracket is a net recipient and every sub-viable single-pool operator is a net contributor (–8 198 ₳/yr at \$0.25/ADA ≈ \$2 050 of annual income stripped from an operator who already cannot cover the cost floor).
  • The operators the reconstructed CPS asks to be supported are funding the reform; the operators already at viability are paid by it.

Why this matters against the missing CPS. §1.4 identifies "the operator growth path is not functioning as intended" as the CIP's foundational claim. A reform that (i) fails to materially improve ROS at the small-pool end under its standalone shape, (ii) under its paired shape actively reduces small-operator revenue while raising large-operator revenue, and (iii) in both shapes amplifies the transfer up the MPO ladder via fleet multiplication, is not a growth-path instrument. It is a fee-schedule redistribution whose monotonicity on both the pool axis and the operator-fleet axis is the opposite of what the CPS requires (CIP-0023.S2) — and that is before composition with the redundancy of §3.1 (CIP-0023.S1). The reform also misses the struggling-pool tail entirely, since the below-floor tiers stay 100 %-fee-consumed under every variant (CIP-0023.S3).

§2.3
OPE.O8.F1
The delegator yield has fallen from 5.3% to 2.0% in 413 epochs (5.5 years), tracking reserve depletion with R² = 0.99. Projection: sub-1.5% within ~1.7 years, sub-1.0% within ~3.5 years. The decline is irreversible without protocol-level intervention — it is built into the monetary expansion formula

§5.1
OPE.O8.F2
At 2.0%, Cardano sits below the USD risk-free rate (4.3%) and at the bottom of the PoS landscape. No other major chain combines this low a yield with liquid, non-custodial, slashing-free design. The low return is the cost of that design — delegation is a conviction bet on ADA, not a yield-seeking decision
The mechanism assumes yield-sensitive delegators, but the yield regime no longer supports that assumption
§5.2
OPE.O8.F3

The 0.39pp retail yield spread compresses proportionally: at 1.0% base yield (~3.5 years), the same relative dispersion produces ~0.20pp — indistinguishable from block-production noise. Pools that are viable today will cross the sub-viable threshold
The failures documented in §4 are not static — they degrade every epoch
§5.3
OPE.O8.F4
The declining yield acts as a selection ratchet against small independent operators. The flat fee is fixed in absolute terms while the epoch pot shrinks — the confiscatory zone expands upward every epoch. Single-pool operators bear the full drag with no fleet to amortise it; multi-pool operators are insulated by horizontal scaling. The structural feedback loop (yield compression → confiscatory expansion → single-pool attrition → delegation migration → fleet concentration) drives the centralisation the mechanism was designed to prevent
The mechanism selects against its smallest operators and reinforces its largest
