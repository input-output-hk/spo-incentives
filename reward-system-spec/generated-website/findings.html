Mainnet Diagnostic

Findings & Observations

Every diagnostic finding paired with its mainnet evidence
2026/04/30 · Nicolas Henin

This page consolidates every finding (a problem statement induced from evidence) together with the observations that support it. Each finding links back to the full reasoning on The Diagnostic — Mainnet Observatory; each observation can be clicked for its full text.

O1The epoch pot is a single-source budgetFees▾

Monetary expansion provides ~99.8% of the pot. Fees cover ~0.19%; self-sufficiency would require 12–16× current capacity. Block production is reliable (η ≈ 0.977).

O2The reserve has crossed its half-lifeSustainability▾

Reserve is half-depleted (13.29B → 6.53B ADA) in 5.5 years. Significant reward pressure expected at epochs 1000–1200 (~2028–2029).

O3The reward mechanism operates at ~44% of its potentialDemand▾

Only ~6.8M of ~15.5M ADA pools pot reaches operators/delegators — the rest returns to reserve. 4.55B ADA cumulative (~70% of current reserve) exists because of this. Root cause: ~17B ADA (~44%) does not participate in delegation.

O4Reward parameters have never been adjustedGeneral▾

$\rho = 0.3\%$ and $\tau = 20\%$ are unchanged since Shelley. Neither has been subject to a governance proposal.

O1Two causes account for 54% of the pools pot returning to reserveMechanism▾

Only 6.79M of 15.53M ADA/epoch reaches operators and delegators — 44% distribution efficiency. The participation gap (unstaked ADA) returns 4.91M ADA/epoch (31.6%), and the unused pledge-incentive budget returns 3.43M ADA/epoch (22.1%, 95.6% of the bonus budget wasted). These two causes together (53.7%) dwarf all others: pledge-not-met confiscation (2.1%), performance (0.5%), oversaturation (0.3%) are secondary.

O2The pledge mechanism is economically brokenMechanism▾

78% of staked ADA sits in pools with pledge ratio < 1%; stake-weighted median ratio is 0.07%. Yield on pledge capital is 0.68%/yr at best (full saturation) — below passive delegation yield of 2.3%/yr. 3.4M ADA/epoch (22% of pot) is reserved for the pledge bonus but returns to reserve unused.

O3The pool landscape is stratified into four tiersMechanism▾

Regular block production requires ~3M ADA stake — the emergent viability boundary. Below 1.1M ADA, the fixed cost exceeds pool reward — operators are in economic loss (1,987 below-viability pools). Only 8 pools reach the saturation threshold ($z_0$ = 77M ADA); the cap designed for 500 pools is nearly inactive. Active stake fills only 56.5% of theoretical capacity — at most 282 pools could saturate. Tier boundaries are dynamic and shift with active stake, fixed costs, and $k$.

O4Multi-pool operators control 75% of staked supplyMechanism▾

85 MPO entities operate 901 pools holding 16.4B ADA (75.4% of participating stake). 48 capital-sufficient MPOs could play the pledge game; 37 cannot. Of the 48, 41 are non-compliant — forfeiting ~550K ADA/epoch in pledge bonus. CEX + IVaaS alone hold 7.4B ADA (19.2% of supply) at structurally zero pledge. 3 exemplary MPOs capture 82% of bonus ADA among pledging entities — the mechanism's output rests on 2 private entities.

O5The independent operator base is far smaller and weaker than it appearsMechanism▾

The 741 healthy pools collapse to 283 independent viable operators once MPO fleet members are removed — the competitive field is 3× smaller than the headline. 78% of independent single-pool stake is non-compliant. 561 marginal single-pool operators partially pledge — the narrow policy-sensitive population. The independent base is in slow structural decline: its share of active stake has fallen from 28.0% to 25.0% since epoch 583.

O6The incentive-responsive field is a fraction of the networkMechanism▾

78 of 85 MPO entities (13.74B ADA, 63% of active stake) are outside the pledge-response path — multi-game optimisation, not calibration failure. CEX cannot pledge custodied funds; IVaaS cannot pledge client assets; community fleets choose not to — three distinct mechanisms. The filtered proxy (single-pool operators + retained MPOs) holds 7.89B ADA — only 36% of active stake responds to the pledge signal.

O1The flat fee dominates operator revenue but operators do not actively set itMechanism▾

The flat fee accounts for 60% of all operator revenue in the retail market; the commission accounts for 40%. 64% of pools still declare the former floor (340 ₳) — 178 epochs after a governance action halved it to 170 ₳. 89.5% declare one of two floor values. The flat fee follows a $1/\sigma$ hyperbola: 47.5% of pool reward at the sub-viable tier, 1.5% at near-saturation. No other major PoS protocol uses a flat fee.

O2The commission market is bimodal with an empty middleGeneral▾

87% of pools set a commission at or below 10%; 12% set ≥ 99% (privatisation). The 89pp range between 10% and 99% contains 12 pools. Four bands emerge: no-commission (170 pools, 17.9%), competitive (658, 69.1%), no man's land (12, 1.3%), privatisation (112, 11.8%).

O321% of productive stake is custodial — three mechanisms, three economicsMechanism▾

79 entities operating 143 pools (4.55B ADA, 21.1%) are custodial: by pledge (10 entities, 1.59B), by extraction (57 entities, 2.04B), by delegation (15 entities, 0.92B). Each mechanism produces a different economic outcome — from 1.76M ₳/yr median (custodial-by-pledge) to 29K ₳/yr (custodial-by-delegation).

O4The retail market is 79% of stake and the typical delegator holds 87 ₳Demand▾

809 retail pools, 516 entities, 17.02B ADA, 1,272,836 delegators. This includes institutional operators (Coinbase, Binance, Kiln) — retail by median delegation. The median retail delegation is 87 ₳, remarkably uniform across operator types.

O5Delegators pay 18× more for the same returnMechanism▾

A delegator in a sub-viable pool pays 48.3% effective price for 2.04% net return; a delegator in a near-saturation pool pays 2.7% for 2.34% — 18× the price for 0.30pp of return difference. Net return converges to 1.95–2.34% across the entire retail market regardless of effective price, operator type, or pool size. The return signal is too weak to drive delegation decisions.

O6Operator profitability ranges from 24K to 1M ₳/yr — operators who charge the most earn the leastConcentration▾

A sub-viable single-pool operator absorbs 48.3% of pool rewards but earns 24,820 ₳/yr. An 11+ pool MPO absorbs 7.7% but earns 1,035,496 ₳/yr — 42× more at 6× less effective price. No single-pool operator in the retail market earns a competitive wage: the median ~25,000 ₳/yr ($6,250 at $0.25/ADA) covers infrastructure but not the 5–15 hrs/month of skilled work.

O7Delegation follows visibility, not returnMechanism▾

65.9% of retail delegators sit in hollow MPO pools at 2.18% net return; hollow single-pool near-saturation pools offer 2.34% — 0.16pp more — yet hold only 2.7% of delegators. The pledge premium is negative in the retail data: balanced median net return 1.98% vs hollow 2.08%. The flat fee drag overwhelms the pledge benefit — the incentive mechanism's core assumption fails.

O8The mechanism is on a structural clock: reserve depletion compresses yield, widens the confiscatory zone, and erodes the signal every epochDemand▾

The delegator yield has fallen from 5.3% to 2.0% in 413 epochs (5.5 years), tracking reserve depletion with $R^2 = 0.99$. At 2.0%, Cardano sits below the USD risk-free rate and at the bottom of the PoS landscape. As the epoch pot shrinks, the flat fee (fixed at 170/340 ₳) consumes a growing share of pool rewards — the confiscatory zone expands upward every epoch. The yield spread compresses proportionally: at 1.0% base yield (~3.5 years), the same relative dispersion produces ~0.20pp — indistinguishable from noise. The declining yield acts as a selection ratchet against small independent operators: single-pool operators bear the full drag with no fleet to amortise it, driving the centralisation the mechanism was designed to prevent.

O1The productive pool landscape is highly concentrated and stableMechanism▾

Two-thirds of registered pools (1,926 of 2,877) sit below the production threshold — 0.86% of stake, economically irrelevant (F3.1). 73 named entities control 75.5% of productive stake through 464 pools (F3.2). The productive set is a quasi-equilibrium at ~950 pools since epoch 300, with 1.7% turnover per epoch (F3.3). 12 entities with 11+ pools control 40.4% of productive stake (F3.5). CEX + IVaaS (10 entities, 181 pools) hold 7.40B ADA — 34.3% of productive stake at structurally zero pledge (F3.6).

O2Pool size variability is an institutional rebalancing phenomenonConcentration▾

Custodial-by-delegation pools have median CV 19.3% and 21% exceed CV 50%; retail pools sit at median CV 8.4%; custodial-by-extraction are the most inert (median CV 6.6%) (F3.4). Stake volatility is not random — it is segmented by operator type.

O3Stake concentration among delegators is extreme and frozenConcentration▾

The median delegator holds 32 ADA; the mean is 16,055 ADA — a 500× gap (F4.1). 1,000 delegators (0.07%) control 57% of staked ADA; Gini = 0.976 (F4.2). Concentration crystallised by epoch 300 and has not moved since — 9× growth in delegator count without affecting the top-1% share (F4.3).

O4The delegation market has matured and crystallisedConcentration▾

Redelegation fell 75% from 2,000–3,500/epoch (early Shelley) to 600–800 (current regime) (F4.4). The delegator base is structurally bimodal: 42% loyal (201+ epochs), 21% volatile (≤ 5 epochs), 37% moderate (F4.5). Custodial and private pools contribute negligible churn (F4.8).

O5Delegation size determines behaviour, not priceDemand▾

Micro-delegators (< 1K ADA) average 0.67 lifetime switches; whales (1M+) average 3.06 — switching scales monotonically with stake size (F4.6). Whales hold 14.1B of 21.8B staked total, yet only 38% of their stake sits in loyal delegations — capital is disproportionately mobile (F4.7).

O6Yield does not drive delegation decisionsDemand▾

Half of all switches (50.5%) produce zero yield change (±5 bps); the median ROS differential is +0.02 bps (F4.9). Operator take direction is symmetric: 30.8% lower / 37.7% similar / 31.5% higher — no optimisation pattern (F4.10). Pool size is the only asymmetric signal: delegators drift toward larger pools (F4.11). 92.1% of loyal delegations sit in the 0–5% margin range — loyalty and low fees coexist, not trade off (F4.12). Script-based delegation is negligible: 99.97% of delegations are key-based (F4.13).

O7The staking participation rate is structurally decliningFees▾

The staking rate has fallen from 71% (epoch ~260) to 59% (epoch 623) — driven by supply growth outpacing stake inflows (F2.1). 14.36B ADA (39.8%) does not participate; of this, only 134.6M (0.37%) is addressable (registered credential, not delegated). The remaining 14.2B sits in addresses with no stake credential — enterprise addresses (~97% at epoch 376), DeFi scripts, and dormant wallets (F5.1).

O8The submitter population is contracting while transaction volume holdsFees▾

Unique input addresses per epoch peaked at ~512K (epoch 300) and fell to ~158K (epoch 384). The address-to-transaction ratio dropped from 1.47 to 0.48. The chain is not losing activity; it is losing breadth. (F6.1)

O9By headcount, submitters are overwhelmingly stakeable; by fee weight, the picture invertsFees▾

82% of submitter addresses carry a staking credential (base-key, `addr1q`). But 30.6% of fee revenue comes from enterprise and script addresses that structurally cannot delegate. (F6.3, F6.4)

O10Script transactions pay three times the per-tx rate and dominate fee peaksFees▾

Post-Alonzo, script transactions represent 12.6% of count but 29.7% of fees. During high-DeFi epochs, the script share of fees exceeds 40%. (F6.7)

O11Fee revenue is concentrated but less extreme than delegation stakeConcentration▾

The top 10 fee-paying addresses generate 30.5% of all fees; the top 500 generate 51.5%. The concentration is heavy-tailed but below the delegation Gini of 0.976. (F6.6)
